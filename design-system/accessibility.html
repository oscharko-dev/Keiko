Accessibility & readability

Keiko is a tool people stare at for eight, twelve, more hours — in dark rooms and bright ones, indoors and out, across resolutions and devices. Comfort over long sessions isn’t a feature here; it’s the baseline. Toggle the theme (top-left) and read every rule on this page in both.

01 — CONTRAST

Text contrast

Every text tone is measured against the surface it sits on and clears WCAG 1.4.3 — in both themes. The neutral hue (160) keeps the same ramp; chromatic tones darken in light so green, red and amber text stay ≥ 4.5:1 on white. Samples below render in the current theme.

Aa — Keiko 0123Primary · titles, key values≈ 15:1AAA
Aa — Keiko 0123Muted · body, secondary≈ 7.2:1AAA
Aa — Keiko 0123Dim · tertiary, glyphs≈ 5.4:1AA
Aa — Keiko 0123Faint · captions, labels≈ 5.0:1AA
Aa — Keiko 0123Accent text · links, emphasis≈ 5.9:1AA
Aa — Keiko 0123Danger · errors≈ 5.3:1AA
Rule
Minimum 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text, UI borders and the focus ring. The monotonic ramp (muted → dim → faint) preserves hierarchy without any step dropping below AA — every figure here is measured by rasterising each token to sRGB, in both themes. Meaningful accent graphics (connectors, the focus ring) use the darker --accent-text tone so they clear 3:1 on white too. And when the OS asks for more contrast, borders, faint text, the grid and the focus ring all step up automatically.
02 — HIGH CONTRAST

A first-class third mode

High contrast isn’t a bright-room nudge bolted onto a theme — it’s a full, selectable mode that pairs with both appearances (dark+HC, light+HC). Every text tone is lifted to WCAG AAA (≥ 7:1), every UI line to ≥ 3:1, the focus ring thickens to 3px, and status + accent brighten. Switch it from the sidebar — Contrast → Auto / High — or let the OS drive it.

Resolution
Order is enterprise-correct: the OS may raise contrast (@media (prefers-contrast: more)), and an explicit in-app choice ([data-hc="more"]) may raise it too — neither silently lowers it below what the user asked for at system level. The whole system is token-driven, so every component inherits HC for free, and Windows / forced-colors High Contrast hands the focus ring and card edges to the OS palette. Samples above render in the current mode — switch Contrast to High in the sidebar to watch this page step up.

This paragraph is capped at roughly 66 characters per line. Beyond that, the return sweep gets long and the eye loses its place — exactly what you don’t want eight hours in. Capped measure plus 1.5 line-height keeps reading effortless.

06 — ENVIRONMENTS & EYE COMFORT

Built for every room

The same UI has to hold up in a dark studio at 2am and on a laptop in daylight. Both themes are first-class and tuned to reduce strain, not just to invert.

Dark — low light

Surfaces are near-black, never #000 (oklch 0.17). Pure black against bright text causes halation and OLED smear — the warm near-black keeps edges crisp and calm in the dark.

Light — daylight

Background is an off-white (0.955), not pure white, to cut glare; text and chromatic tones darken so contrast holds even with screen reflections outdoors.

Warm-neutral hue

One hue (160) across both themes gives a slightly warm grey that’s gentler over long sessions than a pure, cold neutral.

Follows the user

Appearance and contrast persist per device and apply before first paint — no white flash, no fighting the OS. High Contrast defaults to Auto (follows the system) and can be forced on from the sidebar.

08 — CHECKLIST

Every component must…

Clear 4.5:1 text contrast in both themes (3:1 for large text, borders, focus) — and AAA (7:1) in High Contrast.
Offer a ≥ 24px hit target with non-colliding spacing.
Show a visible focus ring and be fully keyboard-operable.
Convey state with icon + text + shape — never colour alone.
Degrade gracefully under reduced motion.
Carry a label or accessible name for assistive tech.
